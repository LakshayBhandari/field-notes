io vs socket in Socket.IO

io and socket are two different scopes of "who can I talk to". io is the whole server — every connection. socket is one specific connection — the single tab handed to you inside io.on("connection", (socket) => …). io is the switchboard; socket is one phone line on it.

Three calls from the 44Com realtime layer show the whole model:

// MEMBERSHIP — put THIS socket into a room. Sends nothing.
socket.join("agents");

// SEND to everyone in the room, INCLUDING the sender.
// (message route: the send came over REST, no socket to exclude)
io.to(`conversation:${id}`).emit(SOCKET_EVENTS.MESSAGE_NEW, message);

// SEND to everyone in the room EXCEPT the sender.
// (typing: you don't want to see your own "…typing")
socket.to(`conversation:${conversationId}`).emit(SOCKET_EVENTS.TYPING_START);

Mailing-list analogy: socket.join(room) = subscribe me to the list (no email goes out). io.to(room).emit() = email the whole list, me included. socket.to(room).emit() = email the list but not me. You always join first (membership), then emit to later (delivery) — one without the other goes nowhere.

⚠️ IMPORTANT

Common misread: thinking socket.to(room) and io.to(room) are interchangeable ways to "send to a room".

Actually: io.to(room) starts from "everyone" so the sender receives it too; socket.to(room) starts from "this one socket" and means "everyone else in the room but me" — the socket acts as an exclusion. Messages use io.to (the sender wants the saved record back — one render path, and the round-trip proves it persisted). Typing uses socket.to (never echo your own indicator).

Why it matters: a REST handler has no socket to exclude, so io.to is the only option there — and including the sender is exactly what you want. Reaching for socket.to in a place that has no socket is a category error; picking the wrong one gives echoes or silence.
